# Workout planning: evidence and implementation

Reviewed 2026-09-17. The user requested moving the general Coach into workout planning, defaulting to Science based, with a questionnaire and beginner guidance.

## Research translated into behavior

- [ACSM's 2026 position stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/) synthesizes 137 reviews. It supports progressive resistance training, heavier loads for strength, and higher weekly volume for hypertrophy. Its roughly 10-set weekly hypertrophy reference is useful for planning, not a universal optimum or minimum for a novice. Equipment choice and training to failure did not consistently change outcomes across the review.
- [Robinson et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38970765/) found a closer-to-failure association with hypertrophy, while strength improved across a broader effort range. Estimated RIR and exploratory analysis limit precision. The app therefore defaults to 2-3 RIR, 3 for beginners, and 3-4 when tired/sore. Experienced users can train nearer failure without making true failure mandatory.
- [Pelland et al., 2025/2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41343037/) distinguishes direct and indirect weekly sets. The app's logs label broad primary groups, so it reports logged primary sets rather than claiming to measure exact effective volume. A paired unilateral row counts once. Legs cannot be treated as separate, precisely measured quadriceps/hamstring/glute exposure.
- [Singer et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39205815/) reviews inter-set rest. Practical defaults are 2-3 minutes for demanding compounds and 1-2 for smaller isolation exercises, extended when performance needs it. These are programming defaults, not one proven optimal rest duration.

Practical app starting ranges are 2-3 working sets per exercise, often 3-6 per muscle per session, and less for beginners. A beginner may build from roughly 4-8 weekly sets. These session ranges are conservative design choices. Spread volume over available days, consider recent training and readiness, and do not cram a weekly deficit into one session. Failure is optional, especially avoided for new exercises or heavy compounds. The planner explains RIR, warmups, progression and intensity. It never invents an unfamiliar exercise load or novice 1RM.

## Product flow

The bottom bar is Soon (blank placeholder), Summary, Track, History, Profile. Workout contains Plan workout with AI. Three questionnaire pages cover single-session type (Lifting, Cardio, or both), optional muscle focus, goal, style, cardio activity, location, equipment, total time, variety, experience, readiness and limitations. There is no weekly-frequency question. Science based remains the default without the explanatory weekly-set blurb. Goals and training styles are multi-select. Overlapping choices are consolidated into Strength & muscle, Endurance & conditioning, Bodyweight / calisthenics, and Intervals / HIIT. Power, mobility, recovery and general fitness remain distinct goals; bodybuilding and powerlifting express the desired resistance-training emphasis. Multiple selections blend into one session rather than multiplying the session volume. Goal, style, location, equipment and cardio activity offer validated Other text. No equipment is exclusive of all equipment selections. Shared option definitions and validation keep the UI and API aligned. Legacy single-choice preferences and aliases normalize to the new arrays; missing selections and Other descriptions show field-level errors on the appropriate step. Saved nonstandard session times remain visible as selected chips. The removed weekly-frequency profile number has a legacy default and never becomes a new required questionnaire field.

The clearly selected Use my workout history for AI planning control covers preferences and relevant training sent to OpenAI. Other health categories are disabled for planner requests. Preferences are remembered per user on the device; focus/readiness reset on a new visit. New plans use the deep tier. Only the training history tool is exposed and unexpected tool calls are blocked before execution. The screen has no chat composer, conversation list, or message bubbles. The existing backend conversation/action storage is retained for compatibility and provenance. The old profile frequency field remains stored for schema compatibility but is not a questionnaire input or a session-sizing instruction.

The server supplies dated 7/28-day primary-group set counts, recent sessions/sets and cardio. Missing or bounded history is explicitly uncertain; recovery cannot be inferred solely from absence of logs. The planner can fetch more bounded training history. Plans include RIR, rest and technique. Server checks block application when these are missing, set/rep shapes are inconsistent, or effort is inappropriate for a beginner/tired user/unfamiliar movement. Session caps (18 total sets for beginners, 30 otherwise, six per exercise) are conservative application bounds, not scientific optima. The requested session type must match the returned sections. Mixed plans share the total time; a conservative feasibility check includes cardio duration, prescribed between-set rests, two seconds per repetition, and three minutes for lifting preparation/transitions. This is an app rejection heuristic, not a precise workout-duration estimate.

Generate fills the matching local tracker drafts directly and opens Workout for editing. Mixed plans populate both Lifting and Cardio with an explicit shortcut between sections. Existing drafts in affected sections require explicit replacement; unrelated drafts stay intact. Draft schemas are validated before either section is written. Retries reuse the generated proposal after a local write failure. Exercise names, sets, reps, weights, activity, duration and notes remain editable through the existing tracker. No completed history is written until the user saves completed work. Exercise effort/rest/technique stays visible; renaming a movement clears its specific guidance. The lifting log supports repetitions, so the model must not encode seconds of static holds as reps. Recovery/safety responses display a notice without filling drafts.

## Beginner exercise guidance

An expandable exercise guide is available in the editable workout log. Five exact curated movement matches offer brief cues and external ACE instructions/photos: [push-up](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/41/push-up/), [bodyweight squat](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/135/bodyweight-squat/), [barbell bent-over row](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/12/bent-over-row/), [front plank](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/32/front-plank/), and [bent-knee push-up](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/13/bent-knee-push-up/). Links were opened and verified. Other names explicitly say no exact verified guide is available and link to the [ACE library](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/). No fabricated videos, downloaded media, broad substring matching or seeded personal exercise records. AI-generated technique cues are shown separately from curated guidance.

## Deployment

No database migration is needed: existing profile, conversation and action JSON storage supports the extension. The updated coach-chat version 15 is deployed and active on the linked backend with JWT verification enabled. Endpoint checks returned OPTIONS 200 and unauthenticated POST 401. Live model quality and physical-iPhone behavior require separate validation; isolated tests do not establish training effectiveness.
