# Policy source and release workflow

`policies.json` is the single content source for the in-app screens and standalone public HTML. `release.json` records version, review/effective date, owner/contact/territory/audience/monetization decisions and release-review flags. All documents currently remain **development drafts, not effective notices**.

Run `node scripts/build-legal.mjs --preview` from the repository root. It creates `dist/legal-preview/index.html`, seven policy directories and `/licenses/`. Paths are stable relative to the future chosen policy site. HTML uses system fonts, semantic headings and local links; it has no JavaScript, third-party requests, cookies or localStorage. Hosting has not been selected or audited. No page was published.

Documents: Privacy Policy; separate Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy; Terms of Use; Cookies & Similar Technologies; Health & AI Limitations; Support & Privacy Requests; Acknowledgments. In-app access is through signed-out Privacy & Legal and Profile > Privacy & Legal. `third-party-notices.json` is generated by `node scripts/collect-license-notices.mjs`; verify licenses against the final native archive as well.

Before public release, the owner must replace draft statements with reviewed product-specific text, fill all release fields, approve legal/provider/device review and set status to `approved`. `node scripts/build-legal.mjs` refuses unapproved content or remaining draft section headings. The production EAS config has the same release checks; preview/development builds remain usable. These guards prevent an accidental ordinary release, not deliberate bypass through a custom build pipeline. Changing flags alone is not substantive approval.

The final review must establish operator and contacts, available countries and rights/appeals, intended age, provider roles/transfers/retention, backup and incident processes, and whether any specific statutory authorization is required. Do not turn these drafts into mandatory contractual or health-processing consent without that decision. Publishing, provider contracts and service deployment require a separate authorized task.
